# The Comfortable Cage — the two-part edition

The full argument runs to eleven posts across two repositories. Nobody reads eleven posts. These two essays are the argument distilled for a corporate reader — a CTO, a head of engineering, a head of AI — keeping the claims that are backed by evidence and the arguments that are strongest, and leaving out the research agenda, the code, and the practitioner detail.

| # | Essay | What it argues |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | [The Org Chart in the Machine](./part-1-the-org-chart-in-the-machine.md) | Your AI agents are organised like a small software company. The org chart rationed scarce human attention; the scarce resource for a model is context, not calendar. Four in five multi-agent failures trace to roles and hand-offs. We keep the shape for six reasons, three of which buy something real and two of which nobody chose. The alternative is shared cognition, with one wall worth keeping. Six things to do on Monday. |
| 2 | [The Emperor's Toolchain](./part-2-the-emperors-toolchain.md) | Every tool your company runs on was designed for a worker you no longer exclusively employ. Git and languages survive; sprints, review, boards, and the ladder are theatre; databases, email, docs, and the file system are human-shaped all the way down. One principle, one move, a five-layer building, a worked example, and where to start. |

Together they are under 7,000 words.

The long-form series they distil: [The Comfortable Cage](../README.md) (the reasoning layer) and [Prometheus](https://github.com/matteox/Prometheus) (the tool layer). Every claim here has a longer treatment there, with the citations, the counter-arguments, the four architectural patterns in code, and the research agenda.
